# Answer the reader's checklist

**Claim:** a case holds a part for each of the 12 items of the reader's checklist of Runeson 2009, in a fixed order.

- **Grade:** convention; no study shows that meeting the checklist makes a report more trusted.
- **Evidence:** Runeson 2009, Tables 11 to 13; Ralph 2020; ICSE 2003. Where: [baseline](../../evidence/case-reports/baseline.md); [state of the art, Q2](../../evidence/case-reports/state-of-the-art.md).
- **Check:** `shape-conformance` (exact): the case holds each heading of the shape, from Summary to Rerun.
- **Reader styles:** none particular to this genre yet; the reader-ergonomics styles hold.

## What it asks

- The case has the parts of the [shape](../../shapes/case.md): summary, genre, case, why this case, data, analysis, results, chain of evidence, threats, triangulation, roles, lessons, method version and rerun.
- Each part answers an item of the checklist, met or not met with a reason.
- An item not met is named in the Threats to validity, and the case is still published.

## Why

- A reader who was absent asks the same twelve questions of any report; fixed parts let the answers be found.
- Triangulation is the weak item for a report from inside, so the shape asks which sources were not independent.

## Limits

- The check finds the headings, not the answers.
- The checklist assumes a researcher apart from the case; items 48 and 49 are hard from inside.
