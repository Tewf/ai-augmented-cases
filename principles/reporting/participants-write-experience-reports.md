# A participant's report is an experience report

**Claim:** a case written by those who took part names its genre, an experience report with repository measures, and the baseline it meets.

- **Grade:** convention.
- **Evidence:** Ralph 2020, Case Study standard (excludes the experience report from its scope); ICSE 2003, experience-report criteria; Runeson 2009. Where: [frame](../../evidence/case-reports/frame.md); [record 0002](../../decisions/0002-a-case-meets-the-readers-checklist.md).
- **Check:** `case-genre` (exact): the Genre and baseline say experience report and name Runeson 2009, in the case or in the earlier case it links.
- **Reader styles:** none particular to this genre yet; the reader-ergonomics styles hold.

## What it asks

- The Genre and baseline section says that the report is an experience report, and that it has a part drawn from repositories.
- It names the checklist it meets, the reader's checklist of Runeson 2009.
- A later case may take both from an earlier one by a link.

## Why

- The community standard for case studies excludes reports by participants; calling one a case study claims a method it did not use.
- The genre sets what a reader may ask of it: a take-home message, both outcomes, the authors' roles.

## Limits

- The field's name for the genre differs from this repository's. The repository keeps its name; the frame says how.
- No test shows that naming the genre makes a report more trusted.
